# Near-Me Visibility Playbook

## Purpose

Use this playbook to improve qualified visibility in Google Search and Maps for a legitimate local business. It is designed for storefront, hybrid, and service-area businesses.

The goal is not to promise a #1 ranking. The goal is to improve the signals Google publicly describes—relevance, distance, and prominence—while increasing calls, forms, bookings, and other qualified leads.

## Guardrails

- Represent the business exactly as it operates in the real world.
- Do not use a PO box, remote mailbox, or unstaffed virtual office as a Google Business Profile location.
- Show an address only when customers can visit the location during stated hours and the location meets Google's signage and staffing requirements.
- A service-area business that does not receive customers at its address must hide that address.
- Do not simulate searches, clicks, directions requests, visits, reviews, or other engagement.
- Ask all eligible customers for honest reviews without incentives, pressure, gating, or required wording.
- Build a location page only when the business genuinely serves the area and can publish information useful to customers in that area.
- Treat rankings, traffic, and leads as separate measures. A ranking gain is not a business result until it produces qualified demand.

## Inputs

Collect these before recommending changes:

- Business name and website
- Google Business Profile URL
- Business model: storefront, service-area, or hybrid
- Real operating locations and whether customers can visit each one
- Core services and highest-margin services
- Legitimate service areas and realistic travel times
- Primary customer types
- Current website platform
- Access to GBP Performance, Search Console, analytics, and call/form tracking
- Known seasonality, capacity constraints, and commercial priorities

If the business type, location eligibility, or served areas are unclear, stop and resolve those facts before changing GBP or creating location pages.

## Phase 1: Establish the Baseline

Record the starting point before making changes.

### Search and Maps visibility

- Track the 3–5 highest-value queries, including service-plus-location terms and natural near-me variants.
- Measure map visibility from a geographic grid, not a single device or office location.
- Record map-pack position, organic position, and the ranking URL separately.
- Note which competitors appear consistently across the grid.

### Website performance

- Record Search Console impressions, clicks, average position, and indexed pages.
- Record organic landing pages that generate calls, forms, bookings, or purchases.
- Check indexability, canonicals, sitemap coverage, mobile usability, and Core Web Vitals.

### Lead performance

- Record monthly GBP calls, website clicks, direction requests where applicable, forms, booked appointments, and qualified leads.
- Use UTM-tagged GBP website and appointment links when possible.
- Document tracking gaps. Do not invent attribution when call tracking, analytics, or CRM data is unavailable.

## Phase 2: Confirm Profile Eligibility and Accuracy

### Choose the correct business type

| Type | Use when | Address treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Storefront | Customers are served at a staffed, signed location during stated hours | Show the real address |
| Service-area business | The business travels to customers and does not serve them at its address | Hide the address and define accurate service areas |
| Hybrid | Customers can visit a legitimate location and the business also travels to customers | Show the address and define accurate service areas |

Do not convert a service-area business into a storefront merely to influence proximity. A new profile or location must represent a real, eligible operation.

### Complete the profile

- Verify ownership.
- Use the real-world business name without keyword stuffing.
- Select the most specific accurate primary category and only relevant secondary categories.
- Add accurate services, hours, special hours, phone, website, appointment link, and applicable attributes.
- Write a useful business description without promotional clutter or unsupported claims.
- Use real photos that clearly show the team, work, equipment, products, and eligible location.
- Remove duplicate, outdated, or inaccurate profiles and listings through the appropriate Google process.

### Evidence to retain

- Screenshot or export of the current profile fields
- Eligibility rationale for each displayed address
- List of categories and services before and after changes
- Date and owner of each substantive edit

## Phase 3: Improve Reputation Legitimately

### Review request process

1. Ask every eligible customer at a consistent point after a real transaction or completed service.
2. Make the request neutral: invite an honest review of their experience.
3. Provide a direct review link or QR code.
4. Do not offer incentives or filter customers based on expected sentiment.
5. Do not require a neighborhood, service, employee name, rating, or other phrase.
6. Respond professionally to positive and negative reviews.

Suggested request:

> Thanks for choosing us. If you have a moment, would you share an honest Google review about your experience? Your feedback helps other customers make an informed decision.

### Ongoing cadence

- Review new feedback weekly.
- Respond promptly and specifically without stuffing keywords.
- Route operational complaints to the responsible owner.
- Track review volume, average rating, response rate, and recurring themes.

## Phase 4: Build a Useful Local Website Architecture

Use three page types:

1. **Core service pages** explain what the business does and remain location-neutral.
2. **Core location pages** explain where a real branch operates or how a legitimate served area relates to the business.
3. **Service-area pages** target a valuable service-plus-location intent and link to both the relevant service and location hubs.

Preferred structure:

```text
/services/{service}/
/locations/{location}/
/locations/{location}/{service}/
```

### Page-deserves-a-page gate

Create a location or service-area page only when all are true:

- The business genuinely serves the location.
- The query represents meaningful customer intent.
- The page has a clear service and location parent.
- The page can contain useful original information beyond swapping place names.
- The page has a valid conversion path.
- The business can maintain the page as facts change.

Consolidate the topic when those conditions are not met. Do not publish batches of near-identical city or neighborhood pages.

### Useful page components

- Accurate title and H1 matching the page's primary intent
- Service process, pricing approach, limitations, and customer fit
- Real service availability for the location
- Location-specific logistics such as travel time, routes, parking, access, or appointment coverage
- Staff, projects, testimonials, or proof relevant to that service and place
- Original photos from real work
- Clear call, form, booking, or quote CTA
- Links to the core service page and core location page
- Appropriate structured data that matches visible content

Map embeds may help users with directions or context, but do not present them as a guaranteed ranking tactic.

## Phase 5: Publish Helpful Profile Content

Use GBP photos and posts to keep the profile accurate, current, and useful to customers.

Good content includes:

- Completed work, with customer permission
- New services, hours, availability, events, or offers
- Team, equipment, products, and real operational updates
- Links to the most relevant page for the customer's next action

Do not claim that EXIF geotags, posting frequency, or location keywords in posts are confirmed ranking levers. If tested, label them as experiments and judge them by visibility and conversion data.

## Phase 6: Strengthen Prominence and Trust

- Keep name, address, and phone details accurate across important directories.
- Prioritize relevant local, trade, chamber, supplier, partner, community, and editorial mentions.
- Earn links through legitimate relationships, useful resources, sponsorships, news, and expertise.
- Remove or correct material inconsistencies that could confuse users.
- Avoid bulk low-quality citation packages, paid link schemes, and private blog networks.

## 30/60/90-Day Implementation

### Days 1–30: Foundation

- Complete the baseline.
- Resolve business-type and address eligibility.
- Correct profile information, categories, services, hours, links, and attributes.
- Fix measurement and UTM tagging.
- Audit core service, location, and service-area pages.
- Launch a policy-compliant review request process.
- Select the first 3–5 high-value page opportunities that pass the quality gate.

### Days 31–60: Build and Learn

- Improve or publish the first approved pages.
- Strengthen internal links between service and location hubs.
- Add relevant proof, photos, FAQs, and conversion paths.
- Publish useful GBP updates when the business has something worth sharing.
- Correct priority citations and pursue a small number of relevant mentions or links.
- Compare map-grid, organic, and lead changes with the baseline.

### Days 61–90: Expand or Consolidate

- Expand only where earlier pages are indexed, gaining impressions, or converting.
- Improve pages that have visibility but weak click or lead performance.
- Consolidate pages that remain thin, duplicative, or cannibalizing another URL.
- Review lead quality and capacity by location and service.
- Set the next quarterly priority from observed results, not activity volume.

## Scorecard

| Area | Primary measures | Review cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Profile accuracy | Completeness, rejected edits, duplicate issues | Monthly and after changes |
| Map visibility | Geographic grid coverage by priority query | Every 30–45 days |
| Organic visibility | Impressions, clicks, ranking URL, indexed pages | Monthly |
| Reputation | New genuine reviews, rating, response rate, themes | Weekly/monthly |
| Conversion | GBP calls, website leads, booked jobs, qualified lead rate | Monthly |
| Business value | Revenue or pipeline by service/location where attributable | Monthly/quarterly |

## Decision Rules

- If visibility improves but leads do not, inspect query intent, listing presentation, landing-page relevance, and conversion friction.
- If calls increase but lead quality falls, narrow targeting and clarify services, service areas, pricing, or qualification language.
- If new pages do not index or earn impressions, stop scaling and improve differentiation, internal links, site quality, and demand mapping.
- If only one part of the map grid improves, investigate distance, competitor strength, and whether the target area is realistic.
- If a recommended tactic conflicts with Google policy, policy wins.

## Explicitly Excluded Tactics

- Mailboxes, PO boxes, or unstaffed virtual offices used as storefronts
- Fake, duplicate, or lead-generation profiles
- Simulated searches, clicks, directions requests, or visits
- Fake, incentivized, gated, pressured, or scripted-keyword reviews
- Thin city or neighborhood doorway pages
- Automated traffic, bot engagement, paid link schemes, or PBNs
- Guaranteed ranking or lead claims
