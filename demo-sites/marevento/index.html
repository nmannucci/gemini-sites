The lay of it

The coast road gives up here

Marevento sits where the coast road gives up and turns inland. The town spills down the rock in terraces — the harbour at the bottom, the old quarter above it, then vineyards, then nothing but stone and wind. Everything arrives by boat or by the one road, and both of them are slow. This is generally considered the point.

There is no cathedral, no famous painting, no reason to hurry. What there is: four hundred and twelve steps between the water and the upper town, a harbour that empties before dawn and fills again by nine, and an unbroken habit of eating outside from April to November. People come for a weekend and rebook for a fortnight.

Where the town divides itself

The working edge

Il Porto

The harbour is the town's clock. Boats go out at four, come back at eight, and the quay turns into a market that is finished by ten. Afterwards it belongs to the cats, the rope menders, and anyone content to sit on a bollard for an hour.

The long beach

Spiaggia Lunga

Nine hundred metres of coarse pale sand, shelving gently, with water the colour of a swimming pool and none of the chlorine. Umbrellas go up in May. The far end, past the rocks, stays empty even in August because nobody wants to walk that far.

The upper town

Città Alta

Four hundred and twelve steps up, and the reward is a square with three trees, one bar, and a view that makes the climb retroactively sensible. The houses here are older, taller, and painted whatever colour was cheapest the year they were last painted.

The rocks

Le Scogliere

South of the beach the coast breaks into slabs and tide pools, thick with agave and wild caper. Locals swim here rather than at the beach — deeper, colder, no umbrellas. Go at seven in the evening, when the rock is still warm and the light goes gold.

On the water

Everyone here has a boat, or knows someone who does

Afternoons, April to October

Day sails

Four hours out along the cape and back, with a stop to swim wherever the skipper decides the water looks right. Eleven boats do this. None of them take more than eight people.

Daily, weather permitting

The 6am fish run

Two boats take passengers on the morning run. You will be cold, you will be in the way, and you will eat better at nine than you have in months. Ask at the harbour the night before.

Any morning

The swim to the rocks

Four hundred metres from the beach's south end to the first slab. Flat water before eight, chop after. There is no lifeguard and no ceremony — people simply do it, and then have coffee.
